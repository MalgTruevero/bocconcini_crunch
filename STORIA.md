# Why it is called Bocconcini

I was building an agent on an old phone, so I would not have to throw it away:
a Samsung S20 Ultra.

Trying to get past the model's limits, I pictured a child who wants to eat an
enormous cake. He has a small mouth: the cake does not fit. If I try to make him
eat it all at once, he chokes.

The answer was to feed it in **bocconcini**.

In Italian, *bocconcini* means small bites. Not mozzarella.

I would like the name to stay. It is not a requirement: it is a courtesy I ask
of anyone who adapts this library to their own needs and republishes it.

My own version is called **Bocconcini_crunch**.

— Grasso Maurizio

## Originale italiano

Stavo costruendo un agente su un vecchio telefono, per non buttarlo: un Samsung
S20 Ultra. Nel voler far superare i limiti del modello, ho immaginato un bambino
che vuole mangiare una torta enorme. Ha la bocca piccola: la torta non ci sta.
Se provo a fargliela mangiare tutta insieme, il bambino soffoca.

La soluzione era fargliela mangiare a **bocconcini**.

In italiano *bocconcini* vuol dire piccoli bocconi. Non mozzarella.

Il nome mi piacerebbe che restasse. Non è un'imposizione: è una cortesia che
chiedo a chi adatta la libreria alle proprie esigenze e la ripubblica.

La mia versione si chiama **Bocconcini_crunch**.

## It was not born as a library

It began as one private agent, written in Italian, because that was the language
I could think in while fixing it. Nothing was meant to be published.

But the problems were not my agent's problems. They were the problems of a small
model on a small device, and anyone doing that work would meet them. So the parts
that got past the phone's limits were pulled out, translated, and given away.

A few Italian words survive in the code. They are not stains to hide. They are
where it came from.
